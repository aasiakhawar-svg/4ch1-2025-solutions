Question 1 · Elements, compounds & mixtures 6 marks

Substances: calcium, fluorine, petrol, silicon, helium, water, neon
💡 Concept
  • Element – one type of atom only. Compound – different elements chemically bonded (fixed ratio). Mixture – substances just mixed, not bonded (petrol is many hydrocarbons).
  • Diatomic molecule – two atoms joined: H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂. Metals are on the left/middle of the periodic table (Group 1, 2…).
  • Crude oil is split by fractional distillation because each fraction has a different boiling point.

(a)(i) Name a compound 1

✅ Model answerwater (H₂O – hydrogen + oxygen bonded)

(a)(ii) Name a mixture 1

✅ Model answerpetrol

(a)(iii) Name a diatomic molecule 1

✅ Model answerfluorine (F₂). Helium and neon are single atoms.

(a)(iv) Name a metal element 1

✅ Model answercalcium (Group 2). Silicon is a metalloid/non-metal.

(b)(i) Technique to separate crude oil 1

✅ Model answerfractional distillation

(b)(ii) One use of kerosene 1

✅ Model answerfuel for aircraft (jet fuel) – also acceptable: heating/lamp fuel.
🎯 Examiner tipWrite the name, not the symbol, when the question says “give the name”. For fractions, learn one use each: petrol → cars, diesel → lorries, bitumen → roads.

Question 2 · Rates & energy changes 13 marks

Magnesium + dilute hydrochloric acid → magnesium chloride + hydrogen (Mg in excess, exothermic)
💡 Concept – collision theoryA reaction happens only when particles collide with energy ≥ activation energy (Eₐ). A hotter solution means faster particles, so more collisions and, more importantly, a bigger share of collisions are successful. Watch the animation:
Cold – slow particles
Hot – fast particles

(a) Meaning of exothermic 1

✅ Model answerA reaction that transfers (releases) heat energy to the surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings increases.
🎯 Examiner tipSay “to the surroundings”. “Gives out heat” alone is risky; “heat is lost” scores nothing.

(b) Complete the equation 1

✅ Model answerMg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂

(c) Sketch graph: volume of H₂ against time 2

💡 ConceptReaction is fastest at the start (most acid) → steep line. Acid is used up, so rate falls → curve flattens. Because the acid is limiting, the reaction stops and the volume stays constant (plateau).
✅ Model answerCurve starts at the origin, steep at first, gradient decreases, then becomes horizontal (flat line).
🎯 Examiner tipStart at (0,0), never let the curve go down, and make the end truly flat. Use a ruler-free smooth curve, not a straight line.

(d) Why higher temperature changes the rate (collision theory) 4

✅ Model answer
  1. Particles gain more kinetic energy / move faster.
  2. So there are more frequent collisions.
  3. A greater proportion of particles have energy ≥ activation energy.
  4. So there are more successful collisions per second and the rate increases.
🎯 Examiner tipNever say “more collisions” and stop. The marks are for energy ≥ Eₐ → more successful collisions. Do not say particles “expand” or that “the acid gets stronger”.

(e) Reaction profile diagram 5

💡 ConceptExothermic → products lower than reactants. Eₐ = arrow from reactants up to the peak. ΔH = arrow from reactants level down to products level (negative).
✅ Model answer (5 marks)① axes labelled Energy / progress of reaction ② reactants line higher, products line lower ③ curve rising to a peak then falling ④ Eₐ arrow from reactants to peak ⑤ ΔH arrow from reactants to products, pointing down, with reactants and products labelled.
🎯 Examiner tipArrows need arrowheads and must start/end at the correct levels: Eₐ stops at the peak, ΔH stops at the products line. Draw the diagram big.

Question 3 · Group 2, isotopes, bonding & electrolysis 16 marks

(a)(i) Meaning of isotopes 2

💡 ConceptProtons decide the element. Neutrons can vary. Mg-24, Mg-25, Mg-26 all have 12 protons but 12, 13, 14 neutrons.
✅ Model answerAtoms of the same element with the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons.
🎯 Examiner tip“Same atomic number, different mass number” does not answer “in terms of subatomic particles”. Name protons and neutrons.

(a)(ii) Relative atomic mass, 1 d.p. 3

💡 ConceptAᵣ = weighted average = Σ(mass number × % abundance) ÷ 100.
Isotopemass × %
2424 × 76.5 = 1836
2525 × 10.5 = 262.5
2626 × 13.0 = 338
✅ Model answer(1836 + 262.5 + 338) ÷ 100 = 2436.5 ÷ 100 = 24.365 → Aᵣ = 24.4
🎯 Examiner tipShow the sum! The unrounded value (24.365) earns a method mark even if you slip later. Divide by 100 (the abundances add to 100).

(b) Insoluble solid 1

💡 ConceptAll nitrates are soluble. Most sulfates are soluble except barium, lead and calcium sulfate.
✅ Model answercalcium sulfate

(c) Ions in magnesium oxide 3

💡 ConceptMg (2,8,2) loses 2 electrons → Mg²⁺ (2,8). O (2,6) gains 2 electrons → O²⁻ (2,8). Both reach a full outer shell.
✅ Model answerMg: shells 2,8 inside square brackets, charge 2+. O: shells 2,8 (8 outer electrons) in brackets, charge 2–.
🎯 Examiner tipMagnesium must show 8 electrons in its outer shell (the third shell is empty and gone). Always put the charge outside the brackets.

(d) Why only aqueous MgCl₂ conducts 5

💡 ConceptConduction needs free-moving charged particles (ions or electrons).
  • Water – simple molecules, no ions.
  • Solid MgCl₂ – giant ionic lattice; ions locked in place.
  • Aqueous MgCl₂ – ions dissolved and free.
✅ Model answer
  • Water is a simple covalent molecular substance with no ions or free electrons, so no charged particles can carry current.
  • Solid magnesium chloride is a giant ionic lattice held by strong electrostatic forces; the ions are in fixed positions and cannot move.
  • Aqueous magnesium chloride: the ions are free to move and carry charge, so it conducts.
🎯 Examiner tipWrite one reason for each row of the table. Say “ions are free to move”, not “ions are free”, and never say “electrons move through the solution”.

(e)(i) Half-equation at the positive electrode 1

✅ Model answer2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ (State symbols not needed.)

(e)(ii) Why this is oxidation 1

💡 ConceptOIL RIG – Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons), Reduction Is Gain.
✅ Model answerChloride ions lose electrons.
🎯 Examiner tipElectrons must be written on the right of an oxidation half-equation, and balance the charges: 2– = 2–.

Question 4 · Ethanol, ethanoic acid & esters 12 marks

💡 ConceptAlcohol → (oxidise) → carboxylic acid. Acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (needs a concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst). Acids react with carbonates to give salt + water + CO₂.

(a)(i) Other reagent 1

✅ Model answer(dilute) sulfuric acid – the dichromate must be acidified.

(a)(ii) Colour change 2

✅ Model answerfrom orange to green
🎯 Examiner tipNot “red to blue” or “clear”. Orange → green is one of the most-tested colour changes.

(b)(i) Complete the equation 2

✅ Model answer2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂
Sodium ethanoate is the salt.
🎯 Examiner tipCheck balance: 2 Na, 2 CH₃COO, 2 H (water), 1 C + 2 O (CO₂).

(b)(ii) Two observations 2

✅ Model answer① fizzing / bubbles (effervescence) ② the solid dissolves / disappears.
🎯 Examiner tipObservations are what you see. Don’t write “carbon dioxide is made”, which is an explanation.

(c)(i) Role of sulfuric acid 1

✅ Model answercatalyst

(c)(ii) Why a water bath 1

✅ Model answerEthanol (and the ester) is flammable, so a Bunsen flame could ignite the vapour.

(c)(iii) How to know ethyl ethanoate formed 1

✅ Model answerA sweet / fruity smell.
🎯 Examiner tipWaft the vapour with your hand; never sniff directly.

(c)(iv) Displayed formula of ethyl ethanoate 2

💡 ConceptName = ethyl (from the alcohol, CH₃CH₂–) + ethanoate (from the acid, CH₃COO–). The ester link is –COO–. “Displayed” means every atom and bond drawn.

Simple view: H₃C–C(=O)–O–CH₂–CH₃, with each carbon showing four bonds and every H drawn.

✅ Model answerDraw: CH₃ part with 3 H on the first C → that C bonded to a C with a C=O double bond → single bond to O → O bonded to CH₂ (2 H) → CH₃ (3 H). Each C has exactly 4 bonds.
🎯 Examiner tipNo “CH₃” shorthand in displayed formulae – draw every C–H bond. Check the C=O and the C–O–C order (reversing gives methyl propanoate, wrong).

Question 5 · Titration & salts 13 marks

💡 ConceptTitration finds an unknown concentration by neutralising an exact volume. Pipette = accurate fixed volume; burette = accurate variable volume. Concordant results lie within 0.20 cm³. Use moles = concentration × volume (dm³) and the equation ratio.

(a)(i) Apparatus for 25.0 cm³ KOH 1

✅ Model answer(25 cm³) pipette
🎯 Examiner tipA measuring cylinder is not accurate enough. Say “pipette” (not “pipet” or “dropper”).

(a)(ii) Why a white tile 1

✅ Model answerTo see the colour change of the indicator (end-point) more clearly.

(b) Burette reading 2

💡 ConceptRead the bottom of the meniscus at eye level. Between 32 and 33 there are 10 divisions, each 0.10 cm³. The liquid sits halfway between the 32.2 and 32.3 marks → 32.25.
final burette reading32.25
initial burette reading4.15
volume of sulfuric acid added (cm³)28.10
🎯 Examiner tipBurettes read downwards (numbers rise as you go down). Volume = final – initial. Write to 2 d.p. ending in 0 or 5, as the question demands.

(c)(i) Concordant results 1

Titration1234
Volume (cm³)27.4027.6527.3027.35
Concordant?✔✘✔✔
✅ Model answerTick 1, 3 and 4. 27.65 is 0.35 above 27.30, so it is more than 0.20 away.

(c)(ii) Mean volume 1

✅ Model answer(27.40 + 27.30 + 27.35) ÷ 3 = 82.05 ÷ 3 = 27.35 cm³
🎯 Examiner tipNever include the non-concordant result in the mean.

(d) Concentration of sulfuric acid 3

  1. Moles KOH = 0.500 × 25.0 ÷ 1000 = 0.0125 mol
  2. Equation 2KOH : 1H₂SO₄ → moles H₂SO₄ = 0.0125 ÷ 2 = 0.00625 mol
  3. Concentration = moles ÷ volume = 0.00625 ÷ (26.50 ÷ 1000) = 0.236 mol/dm³ (0.2358…)
✅ Model answer0.236 mol/dm³ – with the 3 steps shown (3 marks).
🎯 Examiner tipConvert cm³ → dm³ (÷ 1000). Use the mole ratio – forgetting “÷ 2” is the #1 mistake. State the unit.

(e) Pure dry potassium sulfate crystals 4

💡 ConceptThe acid and alkali are exactly neutralised, so the mixture is just potassium sulfate solution. No filtering of excess solid is needed. Crystallise it.
✅ Model answer
  1. Heat the solution in an evaporating basin to evaporate some water (until saturated, crystals start on a glass rod).
  2. Leave to cool and crystallise.
  3. Filter off the crystals.
  4. Wash with a little cold distilled water, then dry between filter papers (or in a warm oven).
🎯 Examiner tipDon’t evaporate to dryness: crystals form on cooling. Never write “filter the solution first”. There is no excess solid here.

Question 6 · Equilibrium & calculations 10 marks

H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)   ΔH = +26 kJ/mol
💡 Concept – Le ChatelierIf conditions change, equilibrium shifts to oppose the change.
  • Temperature ↑ → shifts in the endothermic direction (+ΔH).
  • Pressure ↓ → shifts to the side with more gas molecules; if equal moles, no shift.

(a)(i) Test for hydrogen 1

✅ Model answerHold a lit splint at the mouth of the test tube → squeaky pop.

(a)(ii) What ⇌ represents 1

✅ Model answerA reversible reaction (reaction can go forwards and backwards).

(b) Temperature 500 °C → 600 °C 2

✅ Model answerThe forward reaction is endothermic (ΔH positive). Equilibrium shifts right to absorb the extra heat, so the yield of HI increases.
🎯 Examiner tipState the direction and why (endothermic/exothermic). Don’t say “it speeds up” – that’s rate, not yield.

(c) Pressure 2 atm → 1 atm 2

✅ Model answerNo effect. There are 2 moles of gas on each side (1 + 1 = 2), so changing pressure doesn’t shift the equilibrium.
🎯 Examiner tip“If any” in the question is a hint that no effect is possible. Always count the gas moles before answering pressure questions.

(d) Volume of HI at rtp, standard form 4

① Mᵣ(I₂) = 2 × 127254
② moles I₂ = 50.8 ÷ 2540.200 mol
③ ratio 1 I₂ : 2 HI → theoretical HI0.400 mol
④ 80% yield → 0.400 × 0.800.320 mol
⑤ volume = 0.320 × 24 0007680 cm³
✅ Model answer7.68 × 10³ cm³
🎯 Examiner tipHydrogen is in excess, so iodine limits the answer. Double the moles (ratio), apply the 80% after, then give standard form: one digit before the decimal point.
